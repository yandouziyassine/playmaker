Playmaker Lab

From a TV picture to tactics

Load a broadcast-style picture: players are detected in your browser, 4 taps calibrate the pitch, and the shape appears from above with the AI coach's options. Or point your camera at a TV in live mode. Nothing is uploaded.

1 · Picture

1 · Live camera

Point the camera at a TV showing a match from the main camera angle, and hold it steady (lean it on something). The video is analysed on this device: nothing is uploaded or recorded.

The camera needs a secure page (HTTPS, or localhost while developing). It stops when you leave live mode or switch to another tab or app.

2 · Calibrate the pitch

Press Auto-calibrate: the painted lines found in the picture are matched to the pitch model (both halves are tried), then drag a point if a blue line is off. Or pick 4 landmarks you can see (no 3 on one line) and tap them on the picture in order. "Near" is the touchline closest to the camera, "left" is the goal on the left of the TV picture. A tap snaps to the nearest corner of the painted lines found in the picture (orange diamonds) within about 20 px; drag the point to undo.

    Standing still: no velocities from a single picture.

      Arrows on the map: the top 3 options (1 = best). Success = the pass arrives, the team keeps the ball after the carry, or the shot is a goal. Value = the coach's score, the expected change in net expected goals (xG). The carrier's team attacks, the other team defends; "other" (referee, keepers?) is ignored. Players off the picture are missing, so lanes can look more open than they are.

      Models: logistic and ridge regressions (pass, possession value, shot) and a closed-form pitch control, trained on Bundesliga tracking and event data, Bassek et al. (2025), DFL / Sportec Solutions, IDSSE dataset (CC BY 4.0), and shots from SkillCorner open data (MIT). Weights: coach/models.json.

      Honest limits

      • Only what the camera sees: a TV picture is a partial view, players off screen are missing.
      • Small model (EfficientDet-Lite0, trained on everyday photos): small, overlapping or blurred players are missed, and people in the crowd or on the bench can be detected.
      • Feet = bottom of the box: occlusions and cut boxes move players on the map by a few metres.
      • Team colours come from the average shirt colour and can fail (similar kits, shadows, keepers, referees).
      • The ball is tiny on a TV picture: often missed, sometimes confused with a head or a boot. On the map it is the point under the ball, so a ball in the air is drawn too far from the camera (approximate). The carrier is just the nearest player within 3 m, and the attacking direction is a guess from where each team's deepest player is: check both.
      • Auto-calibration and tap snapping only use straight painted lines on green (curves, worn or shaded lines and heavy occlusion are missed). With few lines in view the match is ambiguous (check the half and the box it chose, or try the other solution), and the fit score only checks the lines it can see.
      • Live camera: positions are approximate (a few metres) and the speeds are rough estimates from the last frames. The calibration is refined from the painted lines every couple of seconds and redone automatically when the camera (yours or the broadcast one) moves, which takes a few seconds and fails when too few lines are in view: then freeze and tap the points. A few detections per second on a phone, players can blink in and out.
