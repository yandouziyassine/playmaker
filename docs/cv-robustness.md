# CV robustness: the Lab pipeline under domain randomisation

The original benchmark (`scripts/cv-bench/render.mjs`, 60 frames) renders every frame with one look: daylight, the
match's real kit colours, one grass, a main broadcast camera with mild blur. Good numbers there say little about a
floodlit evening game in white vs light grey filmed from the touchline and streamed at low bitrate. This page measures
that.

## Method

- `render.mjs --random --seed 1` draws the same real tracking data (DFL / Sportec, CC BY 4.0) under seeded, reproducible
  conditions, and writes them per frame into `gt.json` (`cond`):
  - **light**: day (one hard sun shadow per player, sometimes a stand-roof shadow splitting the pitch), overcast (soft
    blob, low contrast and saturation), floodlit night (dark sky, floodlight glare, four faint shadows per player,
    vignette, more sensor noise); exposure 0.6-1.35 (`expo`: under < 0.8, over > 1.15) and a warm / cool white balance;
  - **grass**: five base colours (lush, yellow, dark, blue-ish, worn), mowing none / vertical / horizontal / checker with
    5-12 m bands, turf speckle;
  - **kits** (`kitSim`): distinct pairs (red / white, blue / yellow, black / white, red / sky blue), similar pairs (white /
    light grey, navy / black, red / orange, maroon / purple) and grass-like pairs (one team in green);
  - **camera** (`cam`): main broadcast, far (main zoomed out, f 950-1200), high tactical (30-40 m up), low touchline
    (6-9 m up, 8-16 m from the line), zoom (f 2800-4000), end zone;
  - **motion blur**: camera-pan blur 0-14 px (`blur`: low < 3, mid 3-8, high >= 8);
  - **compression**: JPEG quality 0.15-0.95 and sometimes a 0.5-0.75 downscale (`compression` heavy = q < 0.5 or
    downscaled);
  - **graphics**: a scoreboard bar (60 %) and a lower-third ticker (25 %); players hidden under them count as occluded
    (not targets), a hidden ball is not a target;
  - **crowd**: density 0.15-1.6 x, colours mixed / dark / in the team colours (`crowdPal: kit`).
- `robust.mjs` renders 150 such frames (148 kept: two picks had no valid frame) and runs the Lab's real pipeline (`bench.ts` -> `detector.ts`, `teams.ts`) in
  headless Chromium for each config, then aggregates per bucket. Metrics as in `run.mjs`: person recall at IoU 0.5
  (targets: >= 50 % in frame, < 60 % occluded), precision, ball recall (IoU 0.3), team purity (best of the two team <->
  cluster mappings over matched outfield players). Frames are scored 2-6 at a time in parallel pages, so no timings here.
- Buckets are small (10-60 frames, 200-1200 targets): differences under ~5 points are noise.

## Analysis

Read with care: synthetic figures, 148 frames, one seed. The deltas between conditions are large enough to trust; the
gaps between neighbouring configs inside one bucket mostly are not.

**The original benchmark was flattering.** Recall falls from 70 % (Lite0) / 80 % (Lite2) on the one-look set to 30 % /
33 % here, and precision from 86 / 94 % to 71 / 82 %. Almost all of that comes from three conditions that the original
set never had; lighting, grass and crowd matter much less.

1. **Motion blur is the biggest failure.** Recall 47-54 % with pan blur under 3 px, 24-29 % at 3-8 px, 5-6 % above 8 px,
   for both models. Part of this is the metric: the truth box is the sharp body, the blurred person is up to 14 px
   wider, so a correct detection of a thin far player can fall under IoU 0.5. But precision collapses too (34-44 %:
   smeared boxes and fragments), so it is not only scoring. Broadcast pans with 8+ px blur do happen during fast
   switches of play; the clip tracker (prediction through bad frames) is the real defence, not the detector settings.
2. **Small players from far / high cameras are mostly lost.** far 9-19 %, high tactical 6-7 % recall vs 60-74 % for
   low and zoomed cameras, where players are 40-150 px. 72 % of the targets in this set are under 40 px and their recall
   is 19-25 %. The 384 / 448 px tiles shrink nothing, but a 15-20 px player is simply below what EfficientDet-Lite was
   trained to see; smaller tiles (more magnification) would cost about twice the passes and were not tried here.
3. **Kits.** Detection does not care much whether the kits look alike (similar 34-39 % vs distinct 33-36 % recall), but
   a team in grass green is a camouflage problem: recall 14 % (Lite0) / 21-23 % (Lite2), precision 56-76 %. The team
   split is where similar kits hurt: purity 97-98 % for distinct pairs, 65-66 % for white / light grey, navy / black,
   red / orange, maroon / purple, i.e. little better than a coin flip (50 %). k-means on the mean torso colour cannot
   separate them; it needs shorts / socks colours, a learned embedding, or a user hint.
4. **Exposure.** Over-exposed frames (> 1.15) lose 8-10 points of recall and 9-15 of team purity (white and light kits
   clip). Under-exposure and night are *not* worse than day in this renderer (night 34-36 % vs day 29-38 %): the day
   frames carry hard sun shadows and half-pitch roof shadows, and the night frames are dark but evenly lit. Real
   floodlit footage has more noise and glare than this, so treat "night is fine" as unproven rather than proven.
5. **Graphics and compression** cost a few points each (a ticker: -5 to -8 recall, partly because a strip of the
   picture is gone; heavy JPEG / downscale: -3 to -4 recall, -6 to -9 precision).
6. **Crowd.** A dense crowd does not create the expected false positives (precision flat at 71-85 % across densities);
   recall is even higher with a dense crowd, which is probably a confound with camera framing, not an effect.

**What changed in the settings (settings.ts).** Lite2 had only been measured at tileMinScore 0.3. At 0.25 it gains
recall everywhere (randomised +2.8, small players +3.0, far cameras +3.6; original set +3.7) for about 3 points of
precision, and F1 rises on both sets (48.9 vs 46.7; 87.3 vs 86.5). `MODEL_PRESETS.lite2` is now 448 px / 0.25, and
`setSettings({ model })` applies the model's preset: before, picking "Accurate" in the Lab kept Lite0's 384 px tiles,
a combination that was never benchmarked. Lite0 stays at 384 / 0.25.

**What did not help: auto-gain for dark frames.** The obvious fix for night, brighten the frame before detection when
its mean luma is below 85 (gain up to 1.8, `default-gain` config, applied in the page by `run.mjs`), lowered recall
from 30.4 to 28.3 % overall and from 33.7 to 29.1 % at night (it amplifies noise and JPEG blocks, and the model
already copes with dim frames). It is not proposed for detector.ts.

**Next steps the data supports**: a tracker-side answer to blur (keep predicting through blurred frames, lower the
detection weight when the frame's sharpness drops); a smaller tile (256-320 px) only when the calibrated camera says
players are under ~25 px (needs the homography, so a detector.ts / cliprun.ts change); team split on shirt + shorts
colours with a confidence that says "teams too similar" instead of guessing.

Reproduce: `node scripts/cv-bench/robust.mjs --n 150 --seed 1 --configs default,lite2,lite2-t25 --jobs 4 --orig`
(about 30 min on 8 cores with 6 jobs; frames in `scripts/cv-bench/robust-data/`, numbers in
`scripts/cv-bench/robust-results.json`).
